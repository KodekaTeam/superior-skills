# Copy by Context

Use this guide when the requested work needs more than a focused wording change. Identify the reader's goal, the surface, the real action or state, and the product facts before choosing words. Follow explicit user requirements and preserve the established voice.

## Choose what the copy must do

| Surface | Reader needs to understand | Check before writing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page or headline | What the offer is, who it serves, and why it matters | Product capabilities, intended audience, evidence for benefits, and the next available action |
| Button or link | What happens after activation | Actual destination, state transition, cost, approval, and whether the action is available |
| Onboarding or instructions | What to do now and why it is necessary | Current step, prerequisites, required inputs, and recovery if interrupted |
| Empty state | Why content is absent and what can be done | First use, filtering, permission, or genuinely unavailable data; whether a useful action exists |
| Error or validation | What went wrong and a safe next step | The cause actually known, whether user input is preserved, and the available recovery path |
| Success or status | What has happened and what comes next | The authoritative outcome, pending steps, and any meaningful delay |

Do not show a promised outcome before the system confirms it. Avoid exposing sensitive details through an error or implying a recovery action exists when it does not.

## Make calls to action honest

Trace the intended interaction when code or a working interface is available. A button that opens an application form should describe that step rather than promising instant approval or access. Distinguish a navigation link from a destructive, paid, or irreversible action. For important consequences, make the action and consequence understandable in the surrounding context; do not hide them to increase clicks.

When the destination or behavior cannot be verified, use only the evidence available and flag the uncertainty. Do not invent a route, button action, pricing condition, or product capability to complete the copy.

## Develop positioning without inventing evidence

Collect the supplied product facts, audience, problem, differentiators, and constraints. Connect a supported capability to a relevant reader outcome without presenting an unverified result as fact. When benefits or audience assumptions are uncertain, label them as proposals for the user to verify; do not present them as validated research, testimonials, or guarantees.

Offer alternatives when the user asks for them or a meaningful positioning decision remains open. Each option should make a distinct choice and remain compatible with the known facts; do not create variations merely by swapping adjectives.

## Handle missing content and references

Use existing approved product content first. If a required fact is unknown, write a narrower truthful statement, identify the missing input, or use a clearly identified sample when the task permits it. Keep authoring notes out of production copy. For explicitly requested design recreation, follow the skill's existing reference-hygiene and visual-fidelity rules: preserve the requested composition when honest content can fit, without copying another product's identity by default.

## Review the result

Check that each headline, instruction, CTA, and state has a distinct job; neighboring text should clarify rather than repeat it. Verify claims against supplied evidence and ensure the text describes the real state or action. Report any proposed assumptions, missing facts, or unverified behavior that materially affect the copy.
