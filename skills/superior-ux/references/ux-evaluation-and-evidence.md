# UX Evaluation and Evidence

## Rank the evidence

Prefer direct evidence relevant to the product and task:

1. observed user behavior and moderated or unmoderated research;
2. product analytics, funnel data, search logs, and support patterns;
3. production behavior, current interfaces, requirements, and accepted decisions;
4. expert walkthroughs and established interaction heuristics;
5. assumptions and analogous products.

Lower-ranked evidence can guide a hypothesis but cannot prove user preference, comprehension, or improved conversion. Record the source, recency, audience, and known limitation of important evidence.

## Run a scenario walkthrough

Choose representative scenarios that cover the primary outcome and material risk. Include realistic content, permissions, prior state, and entry point. Walk each scenario through orientation, decisions, action, system feedback, completion, and recovery.

Check:

- clarity of purpose, location, and next action;
- match between product language and the user's task;
- recognition instead of unnecessary recall;
- consistency of concepts and outcomes across surfaces;
- prevention of costly errors without blocking routine work;
- visibility of status, delay, partial completion, and failure;
- user control, cancellation, correction, and recovery;
- accessibility and responsive constraints that alter task completion.

Connect every finding to an observed step or explicit contract. State impact, affected users or scenarios, evidence, confidence, and the smallest useful recommendation.

## Validate proportionately

Use prototype walkthroughs for early structure, task-based usability sessions for comprehension and completion, analytics for production patterns, and automated or manual tests for implemented contracts. Define success criteria before collecting results when practical.

Do not claim that a flow is intuitive, validated, accessible, or faster solely because an expert prefers it. Report what was tested, with whom or against what evidence, what changed, and what remains uncertain.
